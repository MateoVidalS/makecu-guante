# F9 integration verification

Scope: five-finger runtime/engine safety, browser integration, and operating guides.
No board was flashed and no physical glove movement was performed.

## Rigid-finger regression

Use seed 42, a clean simulator camera range (thumb 0–100 degrees of flex;
other fingers 0–270), servo ranges 20–150 degrees, a mild simulated patient,
three `full_fist` repetitions, and enable rigidity just as ASSIST starts.
At a 1500 or 2200 mA total limit, the original detector can complete an assisted
attempt without a resistance event. The index example reaches about 1.15 A,
below the 1500 mA firmware limit. The regression checks **every attempt**, so a
later successful resistance detection cannot hide an earlier missed stop.

Cause: the first 300 ms of assist current establish the baseline. When a finger
already resists after follow, those samples include its stall load. A detector
requiring an additional rise above that loaded baseline may never fire. Stagger
order changes other motors' contributions; the 0.95 target cap is not the cause.

Fix: retain relative-current detection, and also detect stall-level aggregate
current when the attributed command/vision gap exceeds 0.10 and vision remains
stalled for more than 200 ms. Keep the existing soft-stop behavior (freeze command,
brief hold, then release) and the independent firmware current cut-off. Total
current plus a camera gap is an attribution estimate, not an individual sensor.
No per-finger current measurement or physical force guarantee is claimed.

`src/engine/rigidFinger.test.ts` covers all five fingers, mild/moderate/severe
patients, 900/1500/2200 mA limits, and three repetitions. The new browser spec
covers every finger in moderate and mild models at 2200 mA, so its result depends
on resistance detection rather than a low aggregate overcurrent limit.

## Calibration and empty-glove friction

Runtime validates camera ranges before saving and before starting a session.
Invalid/nonpositive spans and ranges that imply more than 105% of the known
simulator closure are rejected. Real-camera checks use visible observed raw flex,
without assuming simulator anatomy. Endpoint noise has a 5% allowance. This
flags the observed 110% mismatch; it cannot predict an unseen physical endpoint.

Empty-glove friction rejects invalid input before acquiring the runtime. STOP,
overcurrent, disconnect, explicit stop, source replacement and disposal settle the
pending promise. Safety aborts the whole friction measurement and returns no
curves, preserving the aborted stage for the UI. Camera invisibility is allowed
only for this empty-glove flow; patient evaluation's vision gate remains active.
A disconnected transport relies on the firmware watchdog to release.

## Browser coverage

New specs supplement the existing default-plan/history/replay/doctor flow:

- Manual ranges for all five servos; the final Continue stays disabled until every range is set,
  and firmware acknowledgment advances to camera calibration.
- A fake serial board reports three fingers; Setup shows the required-five error
  and remains disconnected. No real USB port is requested.
- Every rigid finger stops in every repetition; results retain its resistance event.
- Healthy five-finger assist starts motors >=150 ms apart, moves at most three,
  stays below 2.2 A, caps targets at 0.95, and releases afterward.
- Ring/little-finger evaluation saves exactly four slow/fast sweeps and opens its
  detail view. A fresh complete synthetic friction baseline isolates subset
  selection from S2's separate measurement/reconfirmation flow.

Browser rigid/stagger checks use production providers, runtime, engine and
simulator, with an isolated typed REST sink and fixed calibration read. The
subset and existing integration specs exercise real backend persistence.
